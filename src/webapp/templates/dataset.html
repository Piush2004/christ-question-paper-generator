Where topic labels come from

For a course with a verified match in the real CHRIST BCA syllabus, each topic is one real syllabus unit, labelled with its actual title — reported as a SOFT target (the unit is verified; the question→unit assignment isn't). For a course with no syllabus match (confirmed case: graph theory), topics are unsupervised clusters of past questions instead — a PREFERENCE, never claimed as an official syllabus unit.

Generation evaluation (preliminary)

Two same-course temporal hold-outs and one related-course transfer case, three seeds, one 7B local model. The constraint-first pipeline produced validator-approvable, correctly formatted papers in 9 of 9 runs; a one-shot LLM prompt produced 0 of 9; retrieval-only generation produced 2 of 9. No near-copies of historical questions were found at any of five similarity levels.

These results are descriptive, not a quality claim — content was not evaluated by a human, and the labelled Bloom data are too inconsistent (Cohen's κ = 0.29) to support a trained classifier, so no learned Bloom model is deployed.

What this system does not claim
  • That it outperforms other LLM-based approaches in question quality — not human-evaluated.
  • That it predicts exam content.
  • That generated Bloom/RBT levels are verified — they are a target, checked only by a verb-lexicon heuristic.
  • That topic coverage reflects the syllabus for every course — only where a real match exists.
  • Anything about programmes or subjects outside this BCA dataset.
